What is AEO? Answer Engine Optimization

🔄 Updated: November 13, 2026📅 July 1, 2026✍️ Scanliq Team⏱️ 12 min read

AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) is the practice of structuring your content so that it answers a question directly and clearly. The goal is for an answer engine, such as a Google AI Overview, a featured snippet, the "People also ask" box, a voice assistant or ChatGPT, to pick your sentence as the answer when someone asks a question. In this guide you'll find how AEO relates to GEO and SEO, what Google officially said about it in 2026, and the steps that make your content answer-ready. You can also test your own paragraph right away with the tool on this page.

In short
  • AEO aims to make your sentence "the best answer to a question".
  • In its 2026 guide, Google defined AEO and GEO and said both are still SEO.
  • The winning formula: heading as a question, first sentence as the answer, then evidence and detail.
  • Chunking content, llms.txt or special schema are not required; Google said so explicitly.
  • Your page must be eligible to show with a snippet: if you use nosnippet, you can't appear in AI Overviews either.

Why Does AEO Matter Now?

Search is moving from a "list of links" to "an answer". The user asks, reads the answer on the results page or in a chat window, and often clicks on no site at all. In that world, the way to be visible is to be the source of that answer.

The practical difference

In AEO, your sentence is often shown as it is; in GEO, your content is combined with other sources, rewritten, and linked as a source. Both need the same foundation: clear, accurate, easily quotable content. For the big picture, see our post on the difference between GEO and SEO.

What Does Google Officially Say About AEO?

In 2026 Google published a guide called "Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search". It defines the terms AEO and GEO and concludes that optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and therefore still SEO. The concrete points from the guide:

  1. No extra requirements

    No special optimization is needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. The page needs to be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet; even then, showing up isn't guaranteed.

  2. No need to chunk content

    According to Google, there's no need to break content into small pieces for AI to understand it, and there's no ideal page length. Its systems can understand multiple topics on one page and show the relevant part.

  3. No special files or schema

    AI-specific files such as llms.txt neither help nor harm in Google Search, because Search ignores them. Structured data isn't required for generative search either, though Google recommends continuing to use it for rich results.

  4. Original content stands out

    Google highlights "non-commodity" content: expert or experience-based perspectives that go beyond the common knowledge anyone could write. It also notes that seeking inauthentic "mentions" isn't as helpful as it might seem.

An important technical detail

To appear in AI features, a page must be eligible to be shown with a snippet. If you use nosnippet or a very low max-snippet value on a page, you also limit that page's chance of being a source in AI Overviews. Use these settings only deliberately, for content you actually want to hide.

How Do You Write Content That Gets Picked as the Answer?

Google says to write good content for people, and that's exactly what works for answer engines too: content that solves the reader's question in the first line. Four habits make the difference. For a broader writing guide, see our guide to writing for AI.

1. Answer first: the first sentence should answer the question on its own

The first sentence under a heading should make sense even when lifted out of context. Openers like "In this article we'll look at ..." give an answer engine nothing to quote. Give the answer first, then explain why and add detail.

2. Make headings the questions users actually ask

Look at your queries in Search Console and at the "People also ask" box in Google. Turn those questions into H2 or H3 headings and write a clear answer under each one. A clean match between heading and answer helps the answer engine find the right passage.

3. Present information in easy-to-extract formats

Give steps as numbered lists, comparisons as tables and definitions as short paragraphs. A commonly recommended length for definition paragraphs is 40-60 words; that isn't a Google rule, just a practical size for a readable answer.

4. Go beyond what anyone could write

Your own data, test results, customer experiences, real price ranges and examples from the field lift your content out of the "commodity" category. If a hundred sites write the same definition, this original layer is what gets you picked. For trust signals, see our E-E-A-T guide.

Common AEO Myths

As AEO has grown popular, unverified advice has multiplied too. The most common claims and what's actually true:

Myth: "You should chunk your content into small pieces for AI."

Google wrote plainly that this isn't necessary and that there's no ideal page length. Sections that make sense to the reader are enough.

Myth: "Add FAQPage schema and you'll get an FAQ box in Google."

Google has not shown FAQ rich results for any site since May 2026. The schema is valid and harmless, but it no longer produces a separate appearance in Google. The real value of an FAQ section is that it answers the user's question clearly on the page.

Myth: "Without llms.txt you can't appear in AI Overviews."

Google Search ignores these files. llms.txt may be useful for some AI tools, but it has nothing to do with being picked as the answer in Google. Details in our llms.txt guide.

Fact: The basics are everything

A page that is crawlable, indexed, open to snippets, answers the question in its first sentence and offers original information is in the strongest position in both classic search and answer engines.

How Do You Measure AEO Success?

According to Google, traffic from AI Overviews and AI Mode is included in the Search Console Performance report under the "Web" search type; there is no separate report. So combine measurement from three sources:

  • Search Console: track impressions and position changes for question-style queries.
  • Manual tests: ask Google, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Copilot your 5-10 target questions every month and note whether you're cited as a source.
  • Analytics: track visits from AI assistants separately. For setup, see our guide to measuring AI traffic.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is AEO?
AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) is the practice of structuring your content to answer a question directly and clearly. The goal is to be shown as the source of the answer in Google AI Overviews, featured snippets, voice assistants and chat-based AI.
Are AEO and GEO the same thing?
They are very close. AEO focuses on being part of the direct answer to a question; GEO focuses on being cited as a source in AI-generated answers. In its 2026 guide, Google defined both and said both are essentially SEO.
Does AEO require special schema or files?
No. Google wrote explicitly that no special schema.org markup or AI-specific files such as llms.txt are needed to appear in AI features. Structured data is still a useful, standard SEO practice for rich results.
Is FAQPage schema still useful?
Google has not shown FAQ rich results since May 2026. The schema can stay as a valid Schema.org type and does no harm, but it doesn't produce a separate appearance in Google. The value of an FAQ section comes from answering questions clearly on the page.
What is the most important step for AEO?
An answer-first structure: the heading should be a question, and the first sentence right below it should answer that question clearly on its own. The prerequisite is that the page is indexed and eligible to be shown with a snippet.
