How to Use It

Work through the list from top to bottom. Each item has a priority label and a short "how to check" note. Tick the items that are already true for your site; whatever stays unticked is your to-do list.

Priority labels

1. Bot Access and Technical Basics: The Foundation of a GEO Checklist

Before AI can recommend a page, it has to be able to read it. Gaps in this group knock you out no matter how good your content is. For details, see our robots.txt guide and our JavaScript guide.

2. Structured Data: Describe Your Page to Machines

Structured data (Schema.org, JSON-LD) states plainly whether your page is an article, a product or a business, and who wrote it. AI uses this to evaluate your page in the right context. For code examples, see our Schema.org guide.

3. Content and Citability

When AI answers, it quotes sentences that make sense on their own, are clear and can be verified. Generic phrases like "quality service" give it nothing to quote. The full formula is in our guide to writing for AI, and you can test an existing article with our free content analyzer.

❌ Not quotable

"You can find useful information about SEO on our site."

✅ Quotable

"GEO is the work of making a site something AI search engines can cite as a source. SEO focuses on rankings; GEO focuses on being part of the answer."

4. Trust Signals: E-E-A-T

On topics like health, money and law in particular, AI looks at who is saying something. Experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness (E-E-A-T) signals set you apart from similar content. For the concept, see our E-E-A-T guide; for practical steps, see our trust signals guide.

5. Measurement and Maintenance

AI visibility isn't something you set up once and forget. A theme update can reset your robots.txt, and a plugin can quietly add noindex to your pages. This group helps you keep what you've gained.

Measurement and maintenance0 / 5

6. llms.txt: Easy, but Don't Overrate It

llms.txt is a proposed standard that summarizes your site for AI. Some AI tools and agents read it; however, Google has said it does not use it for Search or AI Overviews. Adding one takes a few minutes and does no harm, but it doesn't replace the items above. Details are in our llms.txt guide, and our llms.txt generator builds the file for you.

Where to Start? A Day 1 / Week 1 / Month 1 Plan

You don't have to do all 30 items at once. Here is the order we suggest, balancing impact against effort:

Secure access (1-2 hours): check robots.txt, the CDN setting, content in the page source and noindex/canonical; register with Bing Webmaster Tools and submit your sitemap. It takes an afternoon but determines most of the outcome.

Describe your site to machines: add Organization and Article schemas, add FAQPage to your FAQ sections and validate them. Complete your author and contact pages. On your 5 highest-traffic pages, turn the first sentence into a clear answer.

Strengthen content and start measuring: merge pages that answer the same question, add concrete data and sources to key articles, and share a case from your own experience. Pick your test questions and record your first measurement; ask the same questions again a month later.

🧠 Quick quiz: Your articles are excellent, but ChatGPT never cites you. What do you check first?
Correct answer: bot access. If ChatGPT search's bot can't get into your site, your content can't be read, however good it is. Access always comes first on the list.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an AI SEO checklist the same as a GEO checklist?
In practice, yes. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) means making your site something AI search engines can read and cite; "AI SEO" is the more common name for the same work. The 30 items on this page cover both.
Where should I start on the list?
With the bot access and technical basics group. The robots.txt, CDN setting, content in the page source and noindex checks are critical; if they're missing, the other items have no effect.
How often should I go through this list?
Review the whole list every 3 months, and repeat the measurement items (the Search Console report and test questions) every month. Always recheck the access items after site updates.
If I tick every item, am I guaranteed to appear in ChatGPT?
No. The list makes sure your site is technically readable and ready to be cited. AI systems decide which sources to show; the quality and trustworthiness of your content and what your competitors are doing also play a part.
Can I check the list automatically?
Most of the technical items, yes. Scanliq runs 32 checks automatically, including bot access, structured data, sitemap, llms.txt and content signals. Trust items such as author information, first-hand experience and external references still need your own judgment.
